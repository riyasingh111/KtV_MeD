Kt/V and URR Dialysis Adequacy Calculator

Is your dialysis cleaning your blood enough? This free tool turns two blood test numbers and a few details from your session into a simple answer. Doctors use two scores, Kt/V and URR. For most people on hemodialysis three times a week, a Kt/V of at least 1.2 is the minimum goal, and about 1.4 is the aim.[1] Share your result with your care team, because a number is only one part of the picture.

ℹ️ What is Kt/V? What is URR? (tap to read)

Kt/V is a score that shows how much waste (called urea) one dialysis session cleared from your blood. Think of your blood as a pond. Kt/V counts how many “pond-fuls” of water were cleaned. A higher score means more cleaning. For most people on hemodialysis three times a week, the usual minimum is 1.2 and the aim is about 1.4.[1]

URR (urea reduction ratio) is simpler. It is the percent of urea that dropped during the session. For example, 70% means about 7 out of every 10 parts of urea were cleared.

Both come from the same two blood tests: one before and one after your session.

What is Kt/V?

Think of your blood as a pond. Dialysis is a filter that cleans the pond. Kt/V tells you how many “pond-fuls” of water were cleaned during one session. A score of 1.2 means the filter cleaned the pond’s worth of water about one and a fifth times.

  • K = how fast the filter cleans (clearance)
  • t = how long the session ran (time)
  • V = how much water is in your body (volume)

The longer the session or the stronger the filter, the higher the score.

Your kidneys in 4 taps

What do kidneys do?

Healthy kidneys filter your blood. They remove waste and extra water and make urine. They also help control blood pressure and keep your body's salts in balance.

What is urea?

Urea is a waste made when your body uses protein. When the kidneys cannot remove it, it builds up in the blood. Dialysis helps clear it, and Kt/V and URR are two ways to measure how much was cleared.

Why does dialysis need time?

Waste moves out of the blood slowly, little by little. Completing your prescribed dialysis session is important for receiving the intended dialysis dose.

What do the numbers tell me?

Kt/V and URR show how much urea one session cleared. They are only one part of the picture. How you feel, your blood pressure and your fluid control matter too. Your dialysis team will explain what is right for you.

What is the difference between Kt/V and URR?

URR (urea reduction ratio) is the simpler one. It is the percent of waste (urea) that dropped during the session. Kt/V also counts the fluid removed and the urea your body made during the session, so it is more detailed. Both use the same two blood tests.

How Kt/V is worked out (Daugirdas formula)

This calculator uses the widely used “second generation” Daugirdas formula.[2] In simple steps:

  1. Divide the after-BUN by the before-BUN. Call this R.
  2. Take away a small amount for urea your body makes during the session.
  3. Add a bonus for the fluid that was removed, because it also carries urea out.
Show the formula and what each part means

spKt/V = −ln(R − 0.008 × t) + (4 − 3.5 × R) × UF ÷ W

SymbolMeaning
RPost-dialysis BUN ÷ pre-dialysis BUN
tSession time in hours
UFFluid removed, in litres
WWeight after dialysis, in kg

URR (%) = (1 − R) × 100

eKt/V (equilibrated) is a little lower than spKt/V. It allows for urea that creeps back into the blood after dialysis ends (called rebound). The calculator estimates it with a standard rate equation, using your access type.

Simple habits to get your full dialysis

Tap a topic. Small habits add up.

Be there for every minute

  • Arrive on time and stay until the end.
  • Make up for time lost to bathroom trips or machine problems.[1]
  • Do not accept a shorter session just because the unit is busy.
  • Tell staff if your tube or needle site hurts or the flow seems slow.

Keep fluid gain between sessions small

Fluid you gain between sessions has to be removed in the next one. A 2025 review suggests keeping that gain under about 4 to 4.5% of body weight. Helpful ideas include less salt, which cuts thirst, learning with a counsellor, and using phone apps to track what you drink.[3]

Your own fluid limit is decided by your dialysis care team.

Gentle exercise helps

A 2025 review of 31 studies (1,365 people) found that exercise during dialysis, such as pedalling or light resistance work, improved grip strength, walking distance, step counts and tiredness.[4] Always ask your team what is safe for you first.

Food fuels you

People on dialysis often need more protein than people with healthy kidneys. The 2020 nutrition guideline suggests about 1.0 to 1.2 g of protein per kg of body weight a day for stable people on dialysis.[5] A kidney dietitian will adjust this for you.

Ease stress and worry

A review of six trials found that mindfulness, such as slow breathing and meditation, improved anxiety, low mood and sleep in some people on hemodialysis, and appeared safe.[6] It is an extra support, not a replacement for dialysis.

A gentler start for some people

Some people who still pass a good amount of urine start with fewer sessions, then add more over time. A 2025 study compared this “start slow” approach with standard schedules. More people in the gentler group still had some natural kidney function, and bloodstream infections were less common. Because it was a case-control study, it cannot prove cause, and this approach is not right for everyone.[7] Ask your kidney doctor before changing any schedule.
